Map a Multi-City Rail Trip from a Stop List

You already chose the cities. Paris, then Amsterdam, then Berlin, then Prague — nights and order live in a sheet or a notes app. What you want next is an european rail trip map of that list: the shape of the trip on a map you can see and share.

That is a geography problem first. It is not “is there a train Tuesday at 8,” not seat reservations, and not pass-coverage validation. Those jobs belong to Rail Planner, Omio, and the official pass tools. MemoryMap’s job is narrower: your stop list → map, the same list→map wedge as the cruise and road-trip guides.

A rail list is a geography problem first

Multi-city rail planning splits into two moments:

  1. Shape — which cities, which nights, which order feels right on the map.
  2. Trains — connections, duration, reservations, and whether your pass covers the segment.

People often mash those together and open a ticket planner before they have stared at the geography. If the city list is already trusted, map the itinerary first. Then hand the same cities to a connection tool. Mapping the list does not book anything and does not invent a timetable.

What rail tools usually optimize for

Search results for rail itineraries usually push:

  • City route mappers tied to Interrail/Eurail-style planners (add cities, reorder, then verify trains elsewhere)
  • Ticket and connection builders (times, nights, cost)
  • Network / station click maps
  • Official suggested itineraries from the pass brands

Those tools are useful. They are also heavier than “I already have Paris → Amsterdam → Berlin → Prague on a sheet — show me the map.” Spreadsheet drop and Sheets paste for a rail stop list you already keep is still a weak spot in that SERP. MemoryMap sits in that gap: visualize the itinerary from the list you trust.

Plot your rail stop list in MemoryMap

MemoryMap turns a stop list into a trip map.

Ways in:

  1. Type the cities — name the trip, then add each stop with a date and city / country (state when it helps).
  2. Drop CSV or Excel — same columns; Numbers users export to Excel or CSV first. See Plot trip stops from a spreadsheet.
  3. Paste a Google Sheets link shared as “Anyone with the link can view” (open the right tab before copying), or download Excel and drop it. See Map a trip from a Google Sheet.

Columns: first row = headers. Include a date and a place — or city, state, and country. Title and notes optional (overnight, “day trip,” museum day). Latitude and longitude optional; city names can be looked up. Station codes are not required for this vacation-style map — city-style places match the product.

Stop-list tips for rail

  • Prefer city + country (ambiguous names like “Cambridge” or “Valencia” need context).
  • One row per overnight city — or per stop you care to show on the map.
  • Keep travel order in the sheet; MemoryMap plots the list you give it.
  • Don’t invent station codes or fake coordinates for “travel day” rows you do not want plotted.
  • Numbers → export Excel/CSV before drop.

FAQ

Can I plot rail cities from a list without a ticketing app?

Yes for the map. MemoryMap works from typed stops, a dropped spreadsheet, or a viewable Sheets link. It does not require a pass login to draw your cities.

Will this check train times or pass validity?

No. That is not MemoryMap’s job. Use Rail Planner, Omio, or your operator’s tools for times, reservations, and coverage.

Does this replace Rail Planner?

No. Map the list here; verify trains there.

What columns do I need?

Header row, plus date and place (or city / state / country) per stop. Title and notes optional. Lat/lng optional.

US or Japan multi-city rail?

Same list→map idea. Do not treat the product as Europe-only; use clear city + country (or region) names and keep booking in local rail tools.
