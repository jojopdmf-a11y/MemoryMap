Road Trip Map from a List of Cities (Keep Your Order)

You already picked the cities. Memphis before Nashville because of the nights you booked. Flagstaff stays where it is because of the hike, not because a solver said so. What you want is a road trip map from that list of cities — the path of the trip you planned — not a tool that reshuffles stops to shave miles off a delivery day.

That is visualization, not optimization. Say it early so the right readers stay and the wrong tools get skipped.

A road trip list is not a delivery route

Most “map my stops from a list” pages assume you are a driver who needs the shortest or fastest order. Optimizers proudly reorder your cities. Google Maps multi-stop workflows hit stop limits and still leave you dragging pins by hand.

Road-trippers often need the opposite honesty: keep the sequence (by date, lodging, concerts, or “we just want to see X next”) and plot road trip cities on a map so the geography is clear. Share the plan. Keep a simple souvenir view. Drive later with normal navigation.

If your sheet is already in trip order, forcing a shortest-drive reorder fights the plan.

When optimization helps (and when it doesn’t)

Optimization helps when:

  • You have many same-day street addresses and you do not care about order.
  • The job is errands, field stops, or “hit these places somehow.”
  • Miles and time are the only score that matters.

Optimization does not help when:

  • Nights are fixed (lodging, friends, tickets).
  • The sequence is scenic, cultural, or emotional on purpose.
  • You chose Nashville after Memphis because that is the trip — not because a matrix said so.

MemoryMap’s job is the second list. It plots the city list you give it. It is not a route optimizer, and this page will not pretend otherwise.

Plot your city list in MemoryMap

MemoryMap turns a stop list into a trip map.

Ways in:

  1. Type the cities — name the trip, then add each stop with a date and city / state / country.
  2. Drop CSV or Excel with the same columns.
  3. Paste a Google Sheets link shared as “Anyone with the link can view” (open the right tab before copying), or download Excel and drop the file.

Columns: first row = headers. Include a date and a place — or city, state, and country. Title and notes optional (overnight, concert, “long drive day”). Latitude and longitude optional; city names can be looked up. Full street addresses are not required for this vacation-style map.

City-list tips

  • Prefer city + state (and country when needed). Ambiguous names (“Springfield,” “Paris”) geocode better with context.
  • One overnight town or planned stop per row, with dates when you have them.
  • Do not bury the only place name in Notes.
  • Numbers users: export to Excel or CSV before drop.

FAQ

Can I map cities in the order I wrote them?

Yes. MemoryMap plots the list you give it. Keeping your trip order is the default intent of this page — not fighting you with a shortest-drive reorder.

Will MemoryMap reorder for the shortest drive?

No. That is not MemoryMap’s job. If you need optimization, use a dedicated multi-stop optimizer. Do not expect MemoryMap to behave like one.

City names vs full addresses?

City / state / country (or a place column) is enough for a vacation-style road trip map. Street addresses are optional; lat/lng are optional too.

Can I still open Google Maps for driving days?

Yes. Use MemoryMap to visualize the itinerary; use Google Maps (or another nav app) for turn-by-turn on each driving day. Visualization and navigation are different moments.

What if I do want optimization?

Use a route optimizer built for that. This page will not claim MemoryMap recalculates the cheapest mile order.
